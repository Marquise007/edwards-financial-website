What this is

Required minimum distributions are the amount the IRS requires you to withdraw from tax deferred retirement accounts each year once you reach your start age. The amount is your prior year end balance divided by a distribution period set by the IRS for your age.

Missing a distribution carries an excise tax of 25 percent of the shortfall under Internal Revenue Code section 4974, reduced to 10 percent if corrected within the correction window on Form 5329.

Roth IRAs have never required distributions during the original owner's lifetime. Since 2024 the same applies to Roth accounts inside 401(k) and 403(b) plans.

Traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs may be aggregated: calculate each separately, then take the total from any one of them. Employer plans may not be aggregated. Each plan's distribution must come from that plan.

The first distribution may be delayed to 1 April of the following year, which stacks two distributions into one tax year. That usually raises the tax bill and can lift Medicare premiums.

Source: Internal Revenue Service Publication 590-B, Appendix B, Table III, effective for distribution years 2022 onward. Start ages under the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022. This is general information and not tax advice. Confirm figures with your tax professional before acting.
