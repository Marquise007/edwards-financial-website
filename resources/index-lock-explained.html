Index Lock & Auto Lock, Explained Clearly

Index Lock is designed to add control, not to predict the market. It can lock in a positive index value before the crediting period ends, while Auto Lock can respond automatically when a preset upper or lower target is reached. Understanding both the added certainty and the tradeoff of locking early is the key.

What to listen for

Focus on when the feature can be used, what it locks, and what changes after a lock is activated.

  1. 1 Manual Index Lock How a positive index value can be locked once during a crediting period.
  2. 2 Automatic targets How preset upper and lower targets can activate a lock automatically.
  3. 3 Control & tradeoff Why a lock can protect a known result while ending participation in later index movement.

Index Lock is available only with eligible allocation options and may be used once per crediting period. A lock is based on the index value at the end of the business day, which may be higher or lower than when requested or when a target is reached. Exercising a lock may produce more or less interest than not exercising it. Policy charges, caps, participation rates, terms, limitations, and product availability vary.
